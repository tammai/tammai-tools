All nine visual types, one product

Every visual below describes the same fictional product — Perk, an expense reimbursement app — so the types can be compared rather than admired separately. Each caption shows the prompt that produced it. Press T to check both themes.

1 · Flowchart

Prompt: make a flowchart of how an expense claim gets approved — receipt upload, OCR, policy check, then either auto-payout or manual review
Hue is doing the explaining before you read a word: the failure path is the only --dg-rose shape, the emphasised step is the only accent one, and the sticky note hangs off a dashed leader with no arrowhead — an arrow would imply it is a step.

2 · Architecture

Prompt: architecture diagram for Perk — mobile clients hit a REST API, a claims service talks to Postgres, an S3 receipt store and OCR workers, and there's an async webhook out to our bank
Layered top to bottom: client, edge, service, data. The dashed group is the trust boundary and the cloud deliberately sits outside it. Both datastores share one hue so they read as a tier, and the dashed edge means async. The bank keeps its amber in figure 3 too — same hue, same system, across the whole document.

3 · Sequence diagram

Prompt: show the request flow when someone submits a claim — mobile app, our API, the OCR service and the bank API, including the returns
Requests are solid and neutral, returns dashed and mint, so the round trips read without following a single label. Activation bars are drawn before the arrows so arrowheads land on top, and every label sits on a --brand-bg chip — without it the lifelines read straight through the text.

4 · Wireframe — desktop

Prompt: wireframe the finance team's claims dashboard — sidebar, search, three stat tiles, a spend chart and a recent-activity list
Wireframes are the one type that drops the hues: --dg-surface / --dg-border greys, with the accent spent once, on the primary action. Copy stays as placeholder bars on purpose — real sentences make reviewers argue about the sentences instead of the layout.

5 · Wireframe — mobile

Prompt: mock up the phone screen for snapping a receipt — capture area, a couple of recent claims, tab bar
Same primitives at phone proportions, in .figure.narrow so it caps at 400px instead of stretching into a billboard. The active tab is the only element carrying the accent.

6 · Org chart

Prompt: org chart for the finance org that owns Perk — VP Finance over Controller, Payroll and Procurement, with their sub-teams
Edges have no arrowheads — reporting is a relationship, not a flow. One hue per level so depth reads at a glance, accent on the root. Each parent's bus is a single path: up from the leftmost child, Q corner, along, Q corner, down.

7 · Mindmap

Prompt: mind map the v1 scope — capture, policy, approvals, payouts, two items each
Centre takes the accent; every branch owns one hue and its stems and leaf rules inherit it. Branches balance left and right so the centre stays centred. Leaves are text on a tinted rule, not boxes — boxing every leaf turns a mindmap into an org chart.

8 · Timeline / Gantt

Prompt: gantt view of the Perk rollout over five weeks — policy audit, design, build, a 50-person pilot, then company-wide. We're mid-week-three.
The column grid is the time axis, and every bar is positioned and sized in column units (x = GX + cw * start) rather than by eye — that is what keeps the bars honest. One hue per workstream, and the today marker is the only accent.

9 · Kanban board

Prompt: draw the current Perk sprint board — backlog, in progress, review, done
Columns are --dg-surface containers; cards sit on --brand-bg so they read as lifted with no shadow anywhere. Hue lives in the tags, never the cards — four columns of coloured cards is noise, and the same tag hue means the same kind of work across every column.

10 · User journey map

Prompt: user journey map for filing an expense — snap, submit, wait, approved, paid. The waiting stage is where people get frustrated.
Stages are columns, lenses are rows. The Feeling row uses faces rather than words so the dip at WAIT is visible before you read anything — that dip is the whole point of the artifact. Opportunities take a hue by sentiment: mint for a clear win, rose where the journey is actively losing people.
